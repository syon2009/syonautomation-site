We migrate to (and from) any of these: Microsoft Dynamics 365 Business Central and Finance & Operations, Acumatica, NetSuite, SAP Business One, Epicor Kinetic, Infor CloudSuite / SyteLine, Sage (Intacct, 100, 300, X3), Odoo, QuickBooks and Exact Macola — plus PLM/PDM, MES / CMMS, historians, and plain SQL, Excel or Access databases.

Both directions

Run the old and new systems side by side during cutover. A cross-reference table maps every old ID to its new one, changes are picked up by date stamp, and clear rules decide which side wins a conflict.

Roll back if it fails

A dry run goes into a test company first. Each live load writes a ledger of every record it created or changed, with a snapshot taken before it starts, so a failed load can be undone record by record. Posted accounting entries can't be deleted in any ERP, so those are reversed with matching entries instead, and moved as opening balances where possible.

No admin rights? Still possible

Data is read the way a normal user can: reports, saved searches, Excel and CSV exports, or read-only ODBC. Loading uses the target's own import templates. Where a step truly needs an administrator, such as a one-time API user in the new system, you get one file and one instruction to hand them.

PLC program migration

Move a PLC program to a new platform

Two very different jobs wear the same name. Same-family upgrades (Rockwell legacy to Logix) are largely automated. Cross-brand conversions (one maker to another) are engineered re-writes, helped by tooling but never push-button. We do both — and we tell you honestly which one you have before you spend a dollar.

Automated — same family

RSLogix 500 (SLC 500 / MicroLogix) and RSLogix 5 (PLC-5) to Studio 5000. A converter maps the N, B, T, C and F files to Logix tags and rebuilds the routines; we then clean up, document and validate against the real I/O.

Assisted — cross-brand

Allen-Bradley, Siemens, Mitsubishi and AutomationDirect (CLICK PLUS / Productivity), in any direction. There is no honest one-click converter between brands — instruction sets, addressing, data types and analog/PID/motion all differ — so we extract your I/O list, tag map and logic structure, then re-implement it natively on the target and test it.

What you get

A working, documented program on the new platform, an I/O and tag cross-reference, a conversion report of every mapped and re-worked rung, and commissioning support. Your old program stays the live one until you sign off.

How we quote

What's in a Syon Automation scope of work

Before any project starts you get an itemized scope of work. Every line says who does what, so management knows exactly which work is ours and which needs other contractors, and in what order.

  1. ObjectiveWhat the finished system must do, in measurable terms.
  2. InclusionsEvery item we supply and perform, listed line by line: programming, HMI screens, drive setup, panel work, testing, documentation and training.
  3. ExclusionsWhat is not in our price, stated plainly so nothing is assumed.
  4. Work by othersItems the plant or other contractors provide, such as electrical installation, conduit and wire pulls, field device mounting, network cabling and switch configuration, mechanical work, permits and inspections.
  5. Site readiness (before we start)Power is live and verified at the panel, field wiring is landed and labeled at the panel terminal blocks, network drops are tested to the panel, and loop and point-to-point checks are complete.
  6. Battery limitsThe exact point where other trades' work ends and ours begins, usually the terminal strip inside the control panel. Problems found on the field side of that point are handled by others or quoted as extra work.
  7. AssumptionsThe conditions the price is based on, such as access hours, existing equipment condition and available documentation.
  8. DeliverablesProgram files, drawings, I/O lists, manuals and a test report.
  9. Testing and acceptanceWhat is tested, how, and what counts as done, at our bench (factory acceptance test) or on site (site acceptance test).
  10. Schedule, milestones and changesDates, payment milestones, and how added work is quoted and approved in writing before it is done (change orders).

Scopes and budgets are estimates based on the information provided and are confirmed after a site visit or review of drawings. Work outside the written scope is quoted separately and done only with written approval.
